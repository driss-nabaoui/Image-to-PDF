## Plate 01: Definition & Essence of Stereotypes
*Source: `WhatsApp Image 2026-11-11 at 21.45.48.jpeg`*

### Extracted Text & Definitions:
- **Definition of Stereotype:**
  > "A stereotype is a vivid but simple representation that reduces persons to a set of exaggerated, usually negative, character traits and it is thus a form of representation that essentializes others through the operation of power. That is, a stereotype suggests that a given category has inherent and universal characteristics and that furthermore these characteristics represent all that such a person is or can be."
- **Structural Formula:**
  > "A stereotype commonly takes the form of a conventionalized idea constructed according to a rigid formula into a clichéd image that labels people."
- **Social Function:**
  > "Stereotyping commonly involves the attribution of negative traits to people who are different from us."

- **5. Racism Defined:**

## Plate 12: Acculturation & Cultural Assimilation
*Source: `WhatsApp Image 2026-11-11 at 21.45.51 (2).jpeg`*

### Extracted Text & Citations:
- **4. Acculturation Defined:**
  > "Acculturation is defined as 'the process of cultural change that occurs when individuals from different cultural backgrounds come into prolonged, continuous, first-hand contact with each other' (Redfield, Linton, & Herskovits, 1936, p. 146)."
- **Individual vs. Group Levels (Berry, 2003):**
  > "This first-hand contact results in changes at both individual (i.e., values, attitudes, beliefs and identities) as well as group level (i.e., social and cultural systems) (Berry, 2003)."
- **Acculturation Architecture (Arends-Tóth & van de Vijver, 2006b):**
  > "Salient forms of the acculturation process are composed of antecedent factors (acculturation conditions), strategies (acculturation orientations), and consequences (acculturation outcomes) (Arends-Tóth & van de Vijver, 2006b)."
- **5. Assimilation Defined:**
  > "Cultural assimilation is the process in which a minority group or culture comes to resemble a dominant group or assume the values, behaviors, and beliefs of another group."
- **Relationship between Assimilation and Acculturation:**
  > "A conceptualization describes cultural assimilation as similar to acculturation, while another merely considers the former as one of the latter's phases."
- **Additive Acculturation:**
  > "Assimilation could also involve the so-called additive acculturation wherein, instead of replacing the ancestral culture, an individual expands their existing cultural repertoire."

## Plates 13–17: Culture Shock & The Four Sequential Stages
*Sources: `WhatsApp Image 2026-11-11 at 21.45.52.jpeg`, `(1)`, `(2)`, `(3)`, and `21.45.53.jpeg`*

### Extracted Theoretical Definitions:
- **Culture Shock Defined:**
  > "Culture shock refers to feelings of uncertainty, confusion, or anxiety that people may experience when moving to a new country or experiencing a new culture or surroundings. This cultural adjustment is normal and is the result of being in an unfamiliar environment."
  > "Culture shock is a sense of confusion and uncertainty sometimes with feelings of anxiety that may affect people exposed to an alien culture or environment without adequate preparation."
  > "Cultural shock takes place on four stages."

### The Four Stages of Culture Shock:

1. **Stage 1: The Honeymoon Stage**
   > "During this stage, everything is new and interesting. You feel excited and stimulated, and you still feel close to everything familiar back home. During this stage you generally focus on the similarities between your home country and your host country."
2. **Stage 2: The Distress Stage (Crisis)**
   > "Once the honeymoon stage wears off, you may suddenly start getting frustrated or annoyed by your new country, specifically the customs and values. The things you're experiencing no longer feel new; in fact, it's starting to feel like the strangeness of a new culture is preventing you from experiencing things."
3. **Stage 3: The Orientation Stage (Acceptance & Recovery)**
   > "The Orientation Stage is the first stage in acceptance. During this stage, you begin to understand why things are done in a certain way. You start to respect the culture and traditions, whether you consider them to be good or bad. You begin to feel more comfortable in your new environment, and you begin to have a more positive outlook."
4. **Stage 4: The Adaptation Stage (Bicultural Functioning)**
   > "During this stage, your attitude changes and you are able to function in both cultures. You have embraced the new culture and are able to see it in a new, yet realistic, light."
